Challenge

Choosing a viable location is one of the most consequential and least accessible decisions a small food and beverage operator makes. Professional site selection consulting in Singapore typically costs SGD 150 to 400 per hour, placing it beyond the reach of many elderly hawker stall operators, whose UNESCO-recognized culinary heritage faces increasing economic pressure. Existing quantitative location methods rely on proprietary mobility data and statistical expertise unavailable to individual operators. Emerging LLM-based location tools generate advice from internal knowledge without validated predictors, a failure mode known as geo-hallucination.

Approach

I built PROSPER, short for PRe-Opening Success Prediction with Explainable Retrieval, a framework that predicts restaurant success using only information available before opening: location, cuisine, price, hours, service options, amenities, and zone context. It combines public Singapore Urban Redevelopment Authority zoning records with a corpus of 1,000 restaurants and 565,689 Google reviews, selected for having complete Google Business Profile data, to construct a Bayesian success label.

Solution

PROSPER trains two predictors on this label. TabPFN is a tabular foundation model that achieves the lowest error of any model evaluated, while LightGBM is deployed for real-time inference. Both substantially outperform two LLM baselines that reason from restaurant attributes with and without retrieved evidence. Each restaurant is embedded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2, and every prediction is attributed with SHAP. Its top features guide a similarity search over these embeddings to surface the most comparable successful and unsuccessful restaurants, whose attributes and reviews are assembled into template-based natural-language narration.

Outcome

The complete framework is deployable at approximately SGD 0.01 per query with high accuracy, interpretability, and accessibility, giving small operators the kind of location intelligence that used to be reserved for well-resourced chains.
